**Search window:** 1 January 2021--29 October 2026\
**Project focus:** Cognitive games, adaptive cognitive training,
clock-drawing assessment, and related digital cognitive assessment.

> **About the five-year rule:** A recent-literature window of
> approximately five years is common in research reviews, but it is not
> a universal academic rule. Keep older seminal papers when they are
> essential to explain a dataset, method, or benchmark. For a review
> with a strict 2021--2026 inclusion criterion, label older foundational
> sources separately rather than presenting them as recent evidence.

> **Verification note:** The references below were selected from

## 6. How to Use These Papers in an Academic Literature Review

For each paper, record:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Field                               What to extract
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Research question                   What problem does the study
                                      investigate?

  Population / dataset                Who or what was studied? What were
                                      the inclusion criteria?

  Method                              Which model, game, intervention, or
                                      assessment was used?

  Outcomes                            Which metrics were measured (e.g.,
                                      MoCA, accuracy, AUC, usability)?

  Main findings                       What did the authors report? Avoid
                                      copying claims without context.

  Limitations                         Sample size, bias,
                                      generalizability, validation, and
                                      follow-up duration

  Relevance to SmritiSetu             What design or experiment can
                                      reasonably be informed by this
                                      paper?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

### Important methodological distinction

-   **Cognitive training:** Games intended to exercise or support
    cognitive skills.
-   **Performance tracking:** Monitoring accuracy, time, errors, and
    changes across sessions.
-   **Screening or prediction:** Estimating risk or a score from data,
    which requires appropriate labels and independent validation.
-   **MoCA:** A standardized cognitive screening instrument. Performance
    on a single game or clock image must not be presented as an official
    MoCA score.

The current clock-image dataset, if it contains only unlabelled images,
can support exploratory computer-vision or annotation work. By itself,
it cannot train a supervised dementia classifier or a model that
predicts MoCA scores. Those tasks require suitable ground-truth labels
and a valid evaluation design.
